Updated competitor comparison — 2026-11-05

The 14 common full datasets are unchanged. All 16 fresh ALP, Gorilla and Camel
measurements are retained separately. Codec work and reruns were performed in
gef-experiments. Backups of earlier plots, tables and reports are in before-*.

ALP: official kernels, native vector/row-group organization, strict IEEE build;
all 16 full datasets pass exact bitwise round-trip validation.
Gorilla: extracted original Facebook Beringei value codec and bit I/O;
all 16 full datasets / 1,482,981,623 values pass bitwise validation.
Camel: authors' original Java codec, configured for four decimal places; normal
16-bit integer differences, documented 19-bit setting on LAT. Count actual bit
writer bytes, not the authors' incomplete getSize counter. Full verification
passes 12 datasets and fails AP/BM/BT/BW. Failing rows are retained visibly
qualified and excluded from lossless frontiers. Aggregate Camel is also excluded.
Falcon: excluded at the user's request; historical raw rows retained only.
BUFF remains the original plain native-access implementation, with a bounded-
precision qualification. The majority/outlier variant is not silently substituted.
Its original selective decoder is unfinished when sparse columns are present.
Fauna/Macaque float32 qualifications remain visible. DAC's sampled exactness and
serialized-size checks found no inflation defect.

Fresh files: alp-corrected-full16.csv, gorilla-camel-original-full16.csv.
Provenance: alp-update-provenance.json, gorilla-camel-update-provenance.json,
manifest.json. Full source/configuration audit: COMPETITOR_AUDIT.txt and
/Users/michelangelopucci/Downloads/gef-experiments/docs/codec-fidelity-20261105.txt.

Plots and aggregate tables use ratios recomputed from integer byte/bit counts.
Gorilla/Camel timing fields are fresh measurements, not reused from the old ports.
Camel timers exclude JVM launch and input transport, following codec-only timing;
its JVM is warmed before measurement. GEF uses its existing tuning harness,
while competitors use LosslessBenchmark. This remains a preliminary cross-harness
comparison, not certification of every historical competitor measurement.
